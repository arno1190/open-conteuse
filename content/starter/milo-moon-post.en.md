## Recording direction / Direction de lecture

Warm, unhurried voice, 85–105 words/minute. Leave 2 seconds between paragraphs and 8 seconds after the listener invitation. Speak the story only: these directions are not narration. No sudden effects; music is optional and none is supplied. Child may listen without answering or moving.

## Story / Histoire

That evening, Milo found an envelope on his pillow. It was empty. On the front, someone had written, “For the Moon. Please fill with something that will not fit inside a box.”

Milo looked under his bed. A sock? That would fit in a box. A pebble? That would too. His yawn, however, floated away before he could catch it.

“That might be a clue,” he whispered.

A little postwoman was waiting outside his window. Her bicycle had two round wheels and a bell shaped like a crescent.

“My name is Feather. Have you got any post?”

“Not yet. How do you send a yawn?”

“You tell its story. The Moon likes letters that help her imagine the Earth.”

Milo picked up a pencil. He drew his wide, round mouth. Then he added the sound of rain on the roof, the smell of warm toast and his neighbour's laugh.

If you were writing to the Moon, which little moment from your day would you choose? You can whisper it, or just think about it.

Milo slipped his drawing into the envelope. But one corner stuck out. He had drawn the laugh much too big.

“Should I fold it?” he asked.

Feather shook her head. “Some laughs travel better out in the open.”

She fastened the envelope to the back of her bicycle. The laugh floated behind her like a ribbon. The bicycle rolled down the path and disappeared around a bend. Milo never saw how Feather reached the Moon. That remained the postwoman's secret.

The next morning, a little reply was waiting on his pillow. There was just a yellow circle and these words: “Thank you. We imagined your laugh up here. It was enormous.”

Milo smiled. He put the letter away in a box. His smile stayed out in the open.

## Away from the screen / Loin de l’écran

With an adult if you like, draw a postcard for the Moon: a sound, a smell or a little moment from your day. Tell its story, then give it to someone or keep it. All you need is paper and pencils.
