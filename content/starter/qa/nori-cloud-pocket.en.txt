Nori had three treasures in her pocket, a feather, a blue button, and a yellow ribbon. She had counted them at the garden door. By the gate she counted four, the fourth treasure sneezed. Sorry, said a very small voice. Between the button and the feather floated a cloud no bigger than a little cake. It had two bumps and looked rather embarrassed. I didn't mean to come in. The wind pushed me. My family went over the apple tree, and now I'm on my own. Nori looked up. Clouds were crossing the garden, but none of them stopped. We'll find a way to get you back. I'm Nori. I haven't got a name yet. I'm only three days old. Nori took out her feather and blew on it. The feather fluttered above her hand, then settled back into her palm. The cloud stayed on her finger. Try being light like that. The cloud flattened itself until it looked like a pancake. It rose a little spun round and landed on Nori's nose. Not quite so close, she laughed. Her nose felt cool. Next she put the blue button on the wall. Perhaps you need a good round shape. The cloud rolled into a ball. This time it floated as high as the gate handle. Then it sank slowly like a bubble that had forgotten how to float. Nori was about to suggest another shape when the cloud whispered. In our family we usually travel as boats. It stretched itself out. A hollow appeared in the middle with a point at each end. Now it was a tiny white boat with a belly to catch the air. Then we need to find some wind, said Nori. She held her yellow ribbon beside the gate. It hung still, by the apple tree it hardly stirred. At the far end of the garden next to the watering can it began to dance. High above them three clouds made big boat shapes. The smallest kept turning back. It was looking for someone. There they are, breathed the little cloud. Nori stayed beside the ribbon. She didn't push her new friend. She waited until it was ready. If you like, imagine the little boat filling its belly. Very gently there's nothing you have to get right. The cloud settled into the moving air. Up it went above the watering can then above the apple tree. One pointed end stretched out to wave to Nori. The three big boats slowed down. The little one slipped between them. Together they became an enormous sailing ship. Nori put the feather and the button back in her pocket. She tied the ribbon to the gate so she could find the moving air another day. Next morning a drop landed on the blue button. Just one. Nori looked up. A little white boat was crossing the garden. Hello for she said. And when she counted her treasures she didn't need to keep every one of them in her pocket.
