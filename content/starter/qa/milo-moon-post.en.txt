Three tiny nox came at Milo's window. Tap, tap, tap. A postwoman stood outside holding a bicycle. Her mailbag shone like a biscuit tin. ''Last post for the moon,'' she called. ''I'm feather, have you got anything to send?'' Milo searched his pocket. Out came a red button, a tissue and a crumb from his afternoon snack. Feather shook her head gently. They've plenty of stones and crumbs up there already. The moon would like to know what makes your family laugh. I'll come back when the big hand points straight down. Off she went, her bicycle bell gave a funny little hiccup. Milo looked at the clock, he had about as long as a bath to make his letter, but how could he put a laugh inside an envelope? First he laughed into a jam jar. Ha ha, quick on with the lid, he pressed his ear against the glass. Nothing, he opened the jar, still nothing except the smell of strawberries. Milo's laugh has escaped or turned into jam. Next he drew his mouth, including every tooth. The picture looked like a zip. Milo stared at it, it wasn't funny at all. In the kitchen Dad pulled out a chair, it made a small, indignant duck noise. Dad stopped, he pulled the chair again. The duck complained again. Milo burst out laughing, so did Dad, the harder they tried to stop, the more the duck seemed to encourage them. This time Milo didn't fetch his jar, he found a big sheet of paper, he drew Dad, the chair and a very surprised duck. Underneath he wrote, in our house, we laugh when the furniture thinks it's an animal. He added feathers hiccuping bell. Then he drew his own laugh, a long yellow path curling all the way around the picture. If you like, imagine a chair that makes a completely different noise. You can keep your idea to yourself. Tap, tap, tap, feather was back, the big hand pointed straight down. Milo showed her the picture, but his yellow laugh stuck out on both sides of the envelope, he tried folding the paper. Dad vanished, he folded it the other way, no more duck. Then Milo fetched a second envelope, he opened its sides and joined it to the first. Now the letter was wide enough for everyone to laugh together. Feather read the words, she looked at the duck. Her bell gave a hiccup and she started laughing too. It works said Milo, even without a jar, the next evening a very long envelope lay on his pillow. Inside the moon had drawn a crater shaped like a chair, a duck sat on it holding a little sign. The sign said again, Milo called Dad. In the kitchen the chair answered first.
