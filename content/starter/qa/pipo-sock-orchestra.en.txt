Someone in the laundry basket kept saying, me first, me first, me first, people lifted a towel. Underneath stood three socks, a red stripey one, a green spotty one, and a purple one with a little hole in its heel. We're giving a concert to find our friends explained red. We've all been separated, if they recognize our music they'll come back. Good idea, said people. Can I help? Red immediately bounced onto the edge of the basket. I'll do the beginning, boom, boom, boom, boom. No me, said green. It tapped its toe twice. Tap, tap, tap, tap. They started together. Red bounced over green's tapping. Green sped up to catch red's bouncing. They sounded like two people trying to squeeze through the same door at once. At the bottom of the basket, purple made no sound at all. People raised a wooden spoon like a conductor. Everyone watched me. One, two, three. The two socks began again louder than before. Red bounced so high it landed on top of the spoon. Green started laughing. The concert stopped before it had properly begun. Let's try quietly, said people. They played quietly, but still at the same time. The little sounds kept pushing each other out of the way. Now it was an argument in whispers. Purple came forward. What about me? What sound can you make? Asked people. Purple took a big breath. It wriggled, puffed itself up and shook its heel. Nothing happened. The air escapes through my hole. I'm no use. Purple looked at the door. What if we listen? People put down the spoon. For a moment nobody spoke, and in that moment two little taps came from behind the door. Tap, tap. Green turned round. That's my sister. She couldn't hear us with all that noise. People looked at purple. Wait, you've just found what we were missing. An empty space so someone else can answer. People placed red on the left, green in the middle and purple on the right. One at a time. Red one big step. Green two little ones. Purple we wait. Boom. Tap, tap. Then silence. If you like you can follow the little rhythm in your head. One big step, two little ones, and a space for an answer. Behind the door came tap, tap. Under the armchair came boom. A purple sock stirred on the washing line. Nobody had noticed it. It had been waiting for its turn, too. People opened the door. Six socks met in the middle of the rug. The reds took one big step. The greens took two little ones. The purples left a lovely quiet space. This time the whole room seemed to know when to answer. As they climbed back into the drawer red asked, who's going first tomorrow? Purple smiled. First we listen.
